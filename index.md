## Understanding the Activated Carbon Filter

The activated carbon filter is a critical component of your RO water purifier, often overlooked but essential for water quality. This filter uses a highly porous form of carbon to trap contaminants through a process called adsorption. The **activated carbon filter** is responsible for removing chlorine, which is commonly used in municipal water treatment. Chlorine can give water an unpleasant taste and odor, and it can also damage the RO membrane over time. Beyond chlorine, activated carbon filters also remove volatile organic compounds (VOCs), pesticides, and other chemicals that can affect the taste and safety of your water. By trapping these impurities, the activated carbon filter significantly improves the aesthetic quality of your drinking water, making it taste fresh and clean. This filter is typically the first line of defense in your RO system, protecting the more sensitive RO membrane from chlorine damage and extending its lifespan. Without a functioning activated carbon filter, your water may have a chemical taste, and your RO system's efficiency will decrease. Understanding the role of this filter highlights why regular maintenance is crucial to ensure it is replaced on schedule, as its adsorption capacity diminishes over time. A saturated carbon filter can no longer effectively remove contaminants, allowing them to pass through to your drinking water. The lifespan of an activated carbon filter depends on the water quality and usage, but it generally needs replacement every 6 to 12 months. In Jaipur's high-TDS environment, the carbon filter may need more frequent replacement due to the higher contaminant load, making regular monitoring essential.
